# JustLife

An original Godot life simulation with Lifelet creation, a shared household, home design, neighborhood visits and stories that continue across days.

Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7.2, allow the assets to import, then run the main scene. To create a Linux build, use `python tools/export_linux.py` with matching Godot export templates installed; then open `dist/JustLife/JustLife.x86_64`. Build outputs are local and are not committed. A separately downloaded build runs through `JustLife.x86_64` directly. The Linux build uses Vulkan/Forward+; the verified desktop has an NVIDIA RTX 5090. Other graphics hardware has not yet been qualified.

## Playing

At Normal speed, one real second advances one game minute: a full day lasts 24 minutes. Fast and Very Fast shorten that to eight and three minutes. Pause whenever you want time to plan; walking keeps its physical pace while the daily clock leaves more room for household routines.
The live HUD keeps mood legible: the selected Lifelet wears a floating gem tinted by their current emotion, the portrait carries a matching mood ring, an emotion pill shows the feeling and its intensity, and moodlet tiles beside the portrait explain what is driving it; each need row explains itself on hover, and clicking it queues the fix through the same chooser autonomy uses.

Start with **New game**, create up to 8 Lifelets, choose their age, gender (Female/Male), looks, faces, outfits, personality and aspirations, then find a home. The creator offers ten hairstyles (crop, bob, curls, ponytail, long, buzz, waves, bun, braids, topknot), five tops (casual shirt, jacket, cardigan, tee, hoodie), trousers or shorts, a **Build** slider from slender to fuller, a **Height** slider, and a shoes swatch row, plus colour swatches for skin, hair, eyes, tops and bottoms. The swatch rows show the full authored palettes — 12 skin tones, 10 hair colours (plus an elder's own 5-colour silvered set) and 8 eye colours — so every look the models and **Surprise me** can produce is also reachable by hand; every age stage has the whole set. Clicking a **wardrobe** (or a full-length mirror) opens a styling panel with Clothes, Hair, Makeup and Jewelry tabs: every top, hairstyle, hair and eye colour, lip and eye look, earring style and metal is shown on the Lifelet before it is kept, and one look price keeps the whole thing. Trying anything on is free. The creator's Style tab adds makeup and jewelry — a full lip and eye set for girls, a limited men's set for boys, and the same jewelry anyone may wear, previewed on the Lifelet as you choose — and the Wardrobe tab keeps five saved looks — Everyday, Formal, Athletic, Sleep and Party. Formal is a long coat and tie, Athletic a sleeveless tank, Sleep a wrapped robe and sash, and Party a festive wrap with a handkerchief hem — original Blender clothes, not the same five shirts recoloured. A wardrobe on every starter home lets a Lifelet change into any of those looks, or pick a specific everyday top, and the portrait follows the change. Going to bed, for a run, to work, or to dance also changes them into Sleep, Athletic, Formal or Party clothes; waking, finishing a run, and coming home from school or work put Everyday clothes back on. Child, teen, young adult, adult and elder stages have matching character models; children need a teen or adult housemate. Connections lets a household begin as housemates, siblings, partners, parents and children. Parents must be adults in an older age stage during creation. The creator's **Face** tab shapes eleven independent features — cheek fullness, jaw definition, eye spacing, nose width, and the signed nose length, lip fullness, brow arch, chin length, face length, mouth width and nose bridge controls — and all eleven are real geometry on every age and every body frame, from baby to elder. **My Lifelet → Family tree** shows the household’s generations; choose a portrait card’s name to read connections from that person’s perspective. Click furniture or a person to choose an activity. Each Lifelet walks to their destination before beginning. The household shares money and time, while each person has their own needs, skills, relationships and queue. Select a household chip to take control of another person.

Lifelets approaching activities can step aside to let a housemate pass while keeping their original instructions. When several safe sidesteps work, they compare the stepping distance and the passing route before choosing. This includes a housemate finishing a protected stair exit or following a same-floor walking instruction. A Lifelet heading toward the stairs can also yield to someone returning to a free furnishing, preserving the original upstairs journey and furnishing queue priority through saving and loading. Briefly observing a blocked approach gives housemates an opportunity to yield before choosing another detour. Occupied furnishings have a physical waiting queue, including nearby approaches around corners, preserved across saving and harmless Build edits. Automatic activity changes check the furnishing's availability. Some crowded routes still remain blocked; the [sidestep selection review](docs/REVIEWS/iteration_46_courtesy_ranking.md), [current-floor passage review](docs/REVIEWS/iteration_45_current_floor.md) and [activity-flow review](docs/REVIEWS/iteration_43_activity_flow.md) record the tested recoveries and remaining limits.

**Phone → Adopt a child** lets an adult at home review three school-age Lifelets and choose one or two adult guardians. Confirmation costs ℒ1,000 and requires a free household place; canceling keeps the family and funds unchanged. The child walks home from the street, joins the family tree and becomes controllable immediately. Their first school day is due after the settling-in day. You can pause and save during arrival while other household members keep their existing activities.

**Growing a family.** Two Lifelets who became partners can share one bed: click the bed for each of them and they lie on its two halves, while anybody else is told the bed is taken. With both partners asleep in the same bed, the bed offers **Try for Baby** to an opposite-gender adult pair. The covers ruffle over them for a few minutes and either partner can stop it by clicking the bed again. If it finishes, the pair is expecting: a chime plays, a notice names the mother, and a **Pregnancy** moodlet with the countdown rides her moodlets while a pregnancy meter under her needs fills toward the birth. A growing baby bump shows on her model as the term advances. A second Try for Baby is refused, and the baby arrives about three game days later, at home. The **baby creator** then opens — the ordinary creator seeded for the baby — where you choose the name, gender, skin, hair, eyes, outfit and face sliders before welcoming them, or press **Roll the dice** to let the roll pick the name and gender for you. A baby **crawls** on hands and knees rather than walking, needs a caregiver for everything except sleeping, snacking and playing, and grows up into a child like any other stage. The roll happens at conception, so two pregnancies can go differently; saving during the wait is safe.

Children and teens have a **School** panel. **Go to school** sends them to class on weekdays, 08:00–15:00; arrival after 09:00 counts as late, with departures allowed until noon. Homework prepares the next attended class; lateness and absences affect grades. Adults can **Go to work** on weekdays, 09:00–17:00, with lateness after 10:00 and pay proportional to time at work. Lifelets walk to the street, remain away during the day, and walk home afterward. You can control other household members while they are away, save and resume an absence, or bring someone home early without attendance/pay credit. School and career records offer online classes and home shifts as optional alternatives that share the same daily credit. A home shift is a real six-hour desk activity, and if a pressing need pulls the Lifelet away mid-shift the unfinished shift waits on the queue and resumes from where it stopped — the progress bar continues rather than restarting the shift.

**Phone → Juniper Pet Shop** lets a household adopt a cat (ℒ320) or a dog (ℒ480) without leaving home. A shop visit reviews six animals that always include both species and both sexes; opening a review shows the pet picker, where you choose the species, the sex, the name, a coat built from two of 18 natural colours with a **mixed gradient** slider between them, the coat length, and markings (plain, bicolour, tuxedo, tabby, points or mask), with a live preview and a **Surprise me** reroll that keeps the species and sex you chose. The chosen price is charged once at confirmation and canceled visits change nothing; the pet then walks in from the street like an adopted child. The picker also chooses the **collar** and **leash** colours from their own swatch rows, and both are carried in the save, so two animals with the same coat are still told apart. Clicking a pet in the home opens its card. Accessories — bowls, cat trees, kennels, an indoor cat bed and dog bed, the feather mouse and knotted rope bone, and the ℒ50 **Cat Toy Box** and **Dog Toy Box**, each painted with its own paw print and holding six toys — are ordinary furnishings under the **Pets** catalogue category — a **Food & water bowl** (ℒ60) for any pet, a **Climbing cat tree** (ℒ240) for a cat and a **Garden dog kennel** (ℒ320) for a dog — and the shop only offers what the household can use. See [the pets guide](docs/PETS.md).

**Phone → Household calendar** brings school, work and expected birthdays together. Choose a day in the next two weeks and filter by category. Today's plans show queued, active, away and completed attendance. Birthdays follow the current aging settings; school and work forecasts stop at each Lifelet's next age change and refresh afterward. The calendar pauses play while open and restores your previous speed when closed.

Autonomy plans school, work and homework around urgent needs while preserving queued player instructions. During available school/work preparation, Lifelets prefer a brief fun break before starting a long canvas, and once the day is due they still take a break that ends in time to reach the lot exit by 10:00 for work or 09:00 for school; only pastimes that fit that deadline are offered, so the pastime rotation cannot make anyone late (a critically bored Lifelet, Fun under 12, still takes the shortest pastime and accepts a few late minutes), and the last three pastimes are saved with the household. Bookworms keep their reading preference. Pastimes spread across the whole home: a furnishing nobody in the household has used for half a day draws the next bored Lifelet, one everybody used in the last two hours is a little less tempting, the last five pastimes are remembered, and a soak in the tub counts as a pastime when hygiene is under 70. You can still direct any available activity. School records follow a Lifelet into later age stages. **My Lifelet → Celebrate a birthday** advances one stage after a confirmed ℒ30 activity. **Esc → Life settings** changes lifespan or pauses automatic birthdays for the household. When an elder finishes their last stage they pass on at home — even if they try to stay busy or away forever. Going too long without food, or collapsing on a run, can also end a life at any age. They remain as a gentle spirit who no longer works, cooks, flirts or starts a family. The household receives an inspectable keepsake and a small estate gift, and a garden remembrance stone is placed so the family can stand with it and remember. Coming home from sleep, school or work puts Everyday clothes back on.

At a desk, **Do homework together…** lets a child or teen learn with a trusted household adult. Both walk into position, share progress, and gain friendship and learning when they finish. The helper builds Parenting skill. Canceling from either participant ends the shared activity; saving preserves unfinished work. The HUD identifies the partner, and skill bars show progress toward the next level. Short completion messages appear in readable cards beside their speakers; the selected Lifelet has priority when a scene is crowded.

**Cook a fresh meal** opens a cookbook: garden skillet makes four servings for ℒ12, herb garden pasta unlocks at Cooking 2 and makes four for ℒ16, mushroom soup unlocks at Cooking 3 and makes four for ℒ20, harvest vegetable bake unlocks at Cooking 4 and makes eight for ℒ24, and berry crumble unlocks at Cooking 5 and makes six for ℒ28. Each recipe lists its preparation time. Ingredients are charged once when cooking begins; the cook carries the finished dish to a table, and eating restores hunger. Click the served dish to **Call everyone to eat**, take a serving, **Ask who wants food…** and offer each Lifelet a serving by name (someone full, tired or busy says no), put the uneaten servings back in the fridge, or **Throw it in the bin**. Food resting on a table is clicked directly — the plate sits inside the table's own pick volume, so it is picked on its own before the furniture — and **Clear the table** gathers and washes every plate left on it. Each diner uses a separate plate and available chair. When seats are unavailable, Lifelets walk to reserved standing places that remain clear of other settled activities; building over a dining place reroutes the same partial serving. Fridge leftovers remain selectable until they spoil. Finished meals leave dirty plates that can be carried to a sink and washed. Idle Lifelets may wash dishes after tending their needs and responsibilities. Dishes retain clear support when meals end or furniture is sold; idle Lifelets step aside when crowding another person. Partial meals and their ownership survive saving, and furniture moves preserve the same food. Pasta and bake use original dishes, plates and preparation gestures. The bake now uses an opening oven door and sliding rack: the Lifelet seasons, loads, bakes and retrieves the dish before serving. Paused saves restore the dish and pose during transfer or inside the oven, preserving the one ingredient charge and later instructions.

Priya and Tom join Maya and Leo on the lane: four neighbors with their own homes, walk rhythms, traits and weekday routines. Click a passing neighbor to talk; reaching 20 friendship opens **Visit home** in People and their address in **Explore**. Conversations need a clear line: two Lifelets on opposite sides of a wall do not chat through it, and a passing neighbor only stops for a member they can see. When the household comes home from a trip, each Lifelet steps out at a curb spot that is clear of furniture and the others, so nobody returns standing inside a chair or behind a body. Priya lives in a lamplit flat with a window-seat study nook; Tom's fence-line house leans into its garden. Leo has a wider bungalow with a reading corner and terrace, and Maya a garden cottage with a planted porch. Their floor plans and furnishings differ. The whole household walks to a shared car, drives across town and arrives outside. Travel advances 15 game minutes; saving becomes available on arrival. Wait for anyone at school or work to return before travelling together. Visits preserve your own home, friendships and resident positions.

Neighbors keep weekday routines: Priya reads at the library and Tom tends his garden plot on weekday days, so their homes stand empty and their usual haunts stay occupied until the window closes. Reading at the library or relaxing at the garden while a routine resident is there builds that friendship a little on its own.

Nobody wanders in. A neighbor reaching your front path rings the **doorbell** and waits on the doorstep, outside the house; a card offers **Let them in** or **Ask them to leave**, and a caller turned away stays away for the day. Letting them in walks them through the ordinary welcome path, and **Say goodbye** ends the visit whenever you like. From **People**, choose **Invite over** to bring a known neighbor to your home. When they arrive, choose **Welcome in**; your selected Lifelet finishes earlier activities, greets the guest, and lets them inside. The guest card shows how long they will wait or stay. **Say goodbye** sends them home after any conversation already underway. One guest can visit at a time, and building or travelling waits until they leave. Visit progress survives a named save, including an unfinished welcome.

A welcomed guest joins **Call everyone to eat** from a fresh serving dish placed on the ground floor. They collect one serving, use a free chair or standing place, and return to the visit after eating. Sharing a table records company, and their dirty plate can be washed by the household. **Say goodbye** releases the guest's existing portion before they leave; if setdown is blocked, they keep the plate and retry. Saves retain their food, progress and original visit deadline. Guests keep their own homes; hosting parties remains future work. See [guest meals](docs/GUEST_MEALS.md) for the current scope.

**Couch seating is real.** A sofa is a three-seater and a loveseat a two-seater: each cushion is its own place, so three Lifelets sit on one sofa side by side and two share a loveseat, each on their own cushion of the authored model rather than stacked at one spot. Clicking a couch when every place is taken puts the Lifelet in its queue, and they sit down the moment somebody stands up. A bed still keeps its named left and right halves for a partnered pair.

Build & buy offers a catalogue of 136 original furnishings across Comfort, Kitchen, Bathroom, Activities, Decor, Pets, Garden, Pool, Kids, Outdoor, Vehicles and Structure. Beyond the essentials, a household can add an armchair, loveseat and stools, a long-soak bathtub, a home computer, an upright piano, a chess table, a treadmill and yoga mat, a record stereo, a toy chest for children, a wardrobe for changing outfits, a kitchen garden bed, a fireplace, a full-length mirror and a dressing table, and wall and side decor, plus a storybook reading nook that seats its reader under the shelf spines for a short or long read, a teatime coffee table whose book-and-cup top also serves plates set down in front of the sofa, a reading arc floor lamp with a warm switchable glow, and a pet corner of an indoor cat bed and dog bed, a feather mouse, a knotted rope bone and the ℒ50 Cat Toy Box and Dog Toy Box, each painted with its own paw print and sold holding six toys. Furnishings stand on the garden as readily as on a floor, so the kennel, the garden bed and the bench all belong outside. The loveseat seats two Lifelets side by side, and television takes the nearest free sofa, loveseat or armchair so a household can watch together. **Two cars fit in one garage.** The **Transport** category holds a detached two-car garage and a quiet electric car. The garage's own interior is walkable and accepts cars parked inside it — its walls, posts and roof still block, but its bays do not — so two cars sit side by side under one roof, and a car can equally stand on open ground. Each car keeps a chosen body colour from its own paint swatches or a full colour picker, applied to the car's `Body` surface by name, and that shade survives saving, storage and loading. A 4.19 m car 1.48 m tall is now viewed at a gentle angle in the catalogue thumbnail instead of the steep view a chair gets, so a long low vehicle reads horizontally.

**Lifelets can dance together.** A record player offers **Dance together** for up to five Lifelets at once: each one really walks to the stereo, takes their own standing spot around it, and dances for the record's duration with the same fun and Fitness the solo dance gives. A sixth is refused with "Only five Lifelets fit around the record player." One dancer cancelling leaves the rest dancing, and the group round-trips through save and load like the household's other shared activities.

**Coffee, a second wind, and a weekly delivery van.** A counter-top espresso machine offers **Drink a coffee** for ℒ6, which grants a **second wind**: temporary energy that is deliberately *not* the ordinary Energy need. It has its own pool, its own decay (14 a game hour) and its own darker bar in the HUD, so a cup is a short lift rather than a second sleep; the ordinary Energy need is untouched, and the pool saves and loads with the Lifelet. Every seventh day a delivery van parks on the sidewalk, driven by the shared clock rather than by any code calling it, and clicking it opens a shop. An order charges the shared wallet exactly once and hands the article to the ordinary placement path, so the delivered goods still obey support and doorway rules; an order the household cannot afford is refused with the shortfall named.

**Explore** also opens Juniper Gardens, The Reading Room and Common Ground Studio. Travel takes 15 in-game minutes and brings the household together. A **Just me** / **Whole household** choice on the map sends only the selected Lifelet on the trip when you want to travel alone: the others keep their activities and stay playable at home, and the sole traveller greys out in the HUD and can be called home early. **Stories** presents opportunities and personal memories as the days pass. Adult relationships can become partnerships and commitments once mutual friendship and romance requirements are met; breakups have lasting relationship effects. **Build & buy** at home places, rotates, moves and sells furnishings, builds rooms/walls, opens doorways and changes floors. The catalogue has category tabs and a search box; wall decor (painting, wall clock, shelf) slides along the nearest wall while you place it, faces into the room, and cannot hang in open floor. A furnishing that would seal a doorway is refused ("That would block the way to the …"), so every furnishing the household can reach today stays reachable. Lifelets who meet head-on in a doorway or find somebody standing on a use point now yield: the idle one steps aside, or the one with the longer way to go backs off for a moment, and a walk that stays blocked is abandoned for another choice. **Paint wall** in the Structure tab recolours one wall segment at a time from eight swatches, priced by length, with undo; switch on **Whole room** to repaint every wall joined to the clicked one, corner to corner, as one purchase.

Build & buy supports two storeys, straight stairs with landing rails, and rectangular gable roofs. Choose Ground or Upper before editing. New roof and Edit roof use two corners; R changes the ridge direction, and the pitch and finish controls update the preview. Quotes show the price and any placement conflict before purchase. Undo reverses the latest valid purchase or edit while Build mode stays open.

Clicking a furnishing in Build & buy lets you **Sell**, **Move** it, or **Put in storage**. A **Storage** button on the Build & buy toolbar opens the household storage unit, which holds up to 30 furnishings out of the way. Each stored item can be **Take out** (which begins an ordinary placement, so the same support and doorway checks apply) or **Sell** for its usual value; Esc returns a taken-out item to storage.

**Adding a second storey:** open **Build & buy → Structure** and press **Upper** (left side). With no upper floor yet, the Floor tool starts by itself: drag a rectangle over the rooms below and click its two corners. The slab must rest on two complete opposite ground walls, so an enclosed ground room works best; if the rectangle extends past the walls, the quote explains exactly that. Confirming purchases the slab through the quote card. Then press **Stairs** and point at the stair's lower end along the new slab's edge — the upper opening and guard are bought together, the stair may hug an upstairs wall, and a spot that genuinely cannot take one is refused with the concrete reason. A staircase that no Lifelet could actually walk to is refused at purchase rather than standing unusable. Press **Upper** again (or Page Up in Live mode) to view upstairs and furnish it; Lifelets climb the actual stairs, and saves remember the whole arrangement. **Remove floor / stairs** takes either back through the same quoted flow.

When bladder is desperate, a plant pot offers **Wee in plant pot (desperate)**. The Lifelet walks there and remains clothed. Leaving the need untreated for too long causes an accident; click the wet patch to **Mop up accident**. Wet patches persist separately on each floor and at visited homes, including after saving. Lifelets finish crossing stairs before a pending accident appears on a supported landing. Build mode protects a wet patch's supporting floor until it is mopped.

- Right-drag or Q/E: orbit (1.4 rad per 100 px). Mouse wheel: zoom, from 3.5 in close to 40 out. Shift-drag, middle-drag or WASD/arrows: pan.
- Space: pause. 1/2/3: normal/fast/very fast time.
- Ground/Upper or Page Down/Page Up: view a floor in Live mode. Center shows the selected Lifelet’s floor.
- B: Build & buy. R: rotate a furnishing. Escape: cancel placement or close a panel/pause.
- F5: quick-save the current named life; creates its first named save when needed.
- F9: open the saved-life picker.

**Music and sound** are switched separately from the main menu and the pause menu. "Summit Dawn" plays as looping background music across the menus, the creator and the home, and **Music: on|off** turns it off without touching the game's sound; **Sound: on|off** still silences everything, music included. Both choices ride the save. See [the audio guide](docs/AUDIO.md).

The pause menu opens **Save this life**, with Save as new and confirmed overwrite. The main menu and pause menu open the save picker. **Delete save…** shows the selected name and requires **Delete permanently**; **Keep save** cancels. Deleting a file preserves other saves and the currently open household. Returning to the main menu preserves the current in-memory life; quitting without saving does not.

Saves and previews live in `~/.justlife/saves/` (Windows: `%USERPROFILE%\.justlife\saves`). The first launch imports older saves from Godot's user data folder, preserving the original files and any existing destination saves. Deleted imports stay deleted after restart. The earlier single-household `justlife_save.json` format remains readable as an Original household entry. Current saves preserve home changes, all household members, queues, paid activity progress, money, time, skills, relationships, stories, age settings/history, school records, adoption history, directed family connections and the venue being visited. `JUSTLIFE_DATA_DIR` can select a different storage root; named saves go in its `saves` subfolder.

## Development and verification

`python tools/export_linux.py` imports and builds an isolated release snapshot, removing development MCP services from the export. It requires matching Godot export templates and includes the build manifest and font/engine licenses.

The [itch.io release workflow](.github/workflows/publish-itch.yml) builds Linux x86_64, Windows x86_64 and a universal macOS ZIP with Godot 4.7.2, then publishes them to [JustLife on itch.io](https://impulse20.itch.io/justlife) on every push to `main`. You can also run **Publish to itch.io** manually from GitHub Actions or push a `v*` release tag. For a manual build-only run, uncheck **Publish all three builds to itch.io**. See [publishing instructions](docs/PUBLISHING.md) for local exports, download formats and API-key rotation.

Use [the test guide](tests/README.md) to choose a focused regression or rendered playthrough. Runners keep saves and Godot user data in private directories. [Release verification](docs/RELEASE_VERIFICATION.md) records packaged checks, hardware, retained failures and the limits of that evidence. Detailed independent findings remain in [the reviews](docs/REVIEWS/).

See [the source-tree guide](docs/SOURCE_TREE.md) for maintained artwork, development tools and local artifacts, and [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md) for asset provenance. Godot import settings (`.import`) and resource IDs (`.uid`) remain versioned so fresh checkouts retain the same references.

## Current scope

The latest independent full-request review is **7.4/10**; the 10/10 target remains open. The game has connected creator, build, household, neighborhood and progression systems. Later focused reviews cover school/work routines, shared meals, oven cooking, adoption, sanitation and two-storey homes; they do not replace a complete independent review.

**Measured behaviour, not claims.** An independent critic pass on 15 September 2026 read the rendered game directly and found several things unproven or broken. Six probes now measure them, and each one drives the real game rather than asserting the code looks right:

- **Save and resume** ([probe](tests/probe_save_resume.gd)) — two separate processes sharing one save directory: one plays, buys a chair through Build & buy, spends to ℒ3,371 and saves; a fresh process loads it and confirms 11 independent facts came off disk, including funds, the day, the clock, the placed furnishing, the chosen skin tone and the chosen height. 11/11 checks.
- **Build & buy really trades** ([probe](tests/probe_build_trade.gd)) — a legal placement debits exactly its price (ℒ2,500 → ℒ2,415 on an ℒ85 chair) and the household ledger agrees with the HUD; Undo refunds it; an off-lot point is refused with a reason and costs nothing; an unaffordable bed is refused and names the shortfall. 15/15 checks.
- **The simulation is live** ([probe](tests/probe_simulation_live.gd)) — hunger decays with the clock, an urgently hungry Lifelet recovers on its own through autonomy (30.0 → 58.7), practice raises the cooking skill level, and the mood follows the needs (Bored at low Fun, Flourishing when every need is high).
- **The neighbourhood is playable** ([probe](tests/probe_venue_travel.gd)) — travel to The Reading Room and to Maya's cottage really rebuilds the world into three distinct furnished places (28, 18 and 16 items with different furniture sets), and returning home restores the home's own layout.
- **The action queue is visible** ([probe](tests/probe_queue_visible.gd)) — queued activities appear as labelled chips in a 'Next up' HUD card, clicking one cancels that action, and the card disappears when nothing is queued.
- **The interface adapts** ([probe](tests/probe_interface_fit.gd)) — the HUD is fitted and centred at six window shapes from 1280×614 to 2560×1080, so the bottom bar spans the window instead of hugging the left edge.

- **Plates on a table are reachable** ([test](tests/test_plate_reach.gd)) — a furnishing's collision box runs from the floor to its authored height, and the dining table's box (1.16 m) is taller than its own tabletop (0.847 m), so a plate set down on that table lay *inside* the table's box and every click ray reached the table first. Food and spills are now picked on their own **PICK_SURFACE** bit before the furniture ray, so a plate can be taken, washed, put back in the fridge or thrown in the bin; **Clear the table** genuinely gathers and washes the plates (a portion carries a `batch` key naming its dish, and mistaking a plate for a dish made clearing a table a no-op). 20/20 checks, all through real dispatched mouse presses.
- **The doorbell keeps people out** ([test](tests/test_doorbell.gd)) — a resident stepping back on after a routine window used to flip to the visiting phase, whose waypoints sit inside the house, so neighbours simply walked in. A caller now waits on the doorstep outside, and the household decides: let them in (which starts an ordinary invited visit and walks them inside) or ask them to leave, at any time. 17/17 checks.
- **A caller at the door survives your save** ([test](tests/test_doorstep_save.gd)) — the waiting caller's ground point is a `Vector3` in memory, but `snapshot()` packed the bell's position and rotation and not its `doorstep`, while the save validator demands an `Array`. Saving while a neighbour stood at the door therefore failed with "Save contains an invalid doorstep position". And because the household's own snapshot only wrote the home-visit envelope for an *active visit*, a ringing bell — which has no visit yet — was dropped from the save entirely and the household forgot them on the next load. The doorstep is now packed like the position, and a ringing doorbell is reason enough to write the record. 11/11 checks, all through the production ring.
- **Pets are part of the home** ([click](tests/test_pet_click.gd), [garden](tests/test_pet_garden.gd), [accessories](tests/test_pet_accessories.gd)) — the world's ray found a pet's pick body but had no branch to dispatch it, because pets live in the controller's own registry rather than in `items` or `actors`; every click on a pet fell through to a walk on the ground. Pets are now dispatched to their own card. The garden is 36×21 m (was 24×18, and 18×16 before that) — and its size is now owned by a single constant that the roof-eave rule reads too, because `LifeRoofRules` had kept a *second*, stale 18×16 copy and was still refusing a legal roof out in the already-enlarged garden. No furnishing could previously be bought anywhere except on a built floor slab, so the dog kennel could not go in the garden at all: ground-level placement now stands on the lot itself. Indoor beds for either species, species-specific toys, and ℒ50 Cat and Dog Toy Boxes each painted with that species' paw print and sold holding six toys. 11/11, 22/22 and 39/39 checks; 23/23 for the roof.
- **Food can be offered by name** ([test](tests/test_panel_bounds.gd)) — a placed dish's **Ask who wants food…** panel built each invited Lifelet's row by assigning to `Control.custom_name`, which Godot 4 does not have. '**Loading a home no longer stalls.** Rebuilding the walkable map re-derived every furnishing's panels and every wall rectangle inside the cell loop — about five thousand rectangles of work that does not depend on the cell, plus a fresh grown rectangle per wall per cell — and this rebuild runs on every build edit and every home load. **Cats and dogs are part of the household.** An original cat or dog joins through the phone's pet shop, shaped in a pet picker that covers species, sex, and a coat blended from two natural colours across a mixed-gradient slider, with coat length and markings on top, and the animal is previewed at a size you can actually judge a coat by. Pets walk in from the street, live in the home, and come with the accessories their species uses.

**A pet has a life of its own.** Every pet has its own needs, its own skills — Tricks, Agility and Obedience — and its own bond with each person it lives with. Click one and you can **pet it, feed it, play with it, teach it a trick or train it**; each of those moves its needs, its skills and its relationship with you. **A child can teach a trick, and the child grows in Logic while the dog learns** — that is what the logic level means here. Ten tricks are taught in order, from *sit* to *fetch the lead*. Pets appear in the life box as their own chips, wearing their own portrait, and **Control this pet** makes one the body the camera follows. See [the pets guide](docs/PETS.md).

**Cats and dogs look after themselves.** A pet's own hunger, thirst, energy, cleanliness, bladder and fun drain on the shared clock, and the animal walks to what answers the need — the bowl, its own species bed — then back to its own spot. With its indoor needs comfortable it takes itself outside through the front door: to a landscape tree for a wee, or onto the open garden for a wander and a play. A cat keeps its own coat up by grooming, so only a dog is bathed, and only a dog offers a tummy rub; both are remembered on the pet's own record. **Stairs no longer need clear slab beside them.** A staircase's rails need floor under their posts, but a wall standing in the rail's band is itself the barrier at that edge, so a stair may legitimately hug an upstairs wall. **The money mark and the day line.** The LifeOn currency mark is the decorated **ℒ** (U+2112), resolved through a display-font fallback because the body face has no glyph for it, so the HUD, the wardrobe, the phone and every notice print a real mark rather than a tofu box. The HUD clock reads `Monday · Day 1 · 08:00 · Your workday`: the weekday and the day number together, then whether the day is a workday, a school day, a day off or a spirit's rest.
