# JustLife

An original Godot life simulation with Lifelet creation, a shared household, home design, neighborhood visits and stories that continue across days.

Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7.2, allow the assets to import, then run the main scene. To create a Linux build, use `python tools/export_linux.py` with matching Godot export templates installed; then open `dist/JustLife/JustLife.x86_64`. Build outputs are local and are not committed. A separately downloaded build runs through `JustLife.x86_64` directly. The Linux build uses Vulkan/Forward+; the verified desktop has an NVIDIA RTX 5090. Other graphics hardware has not yet been qualified.

## Playing

At Normal speed, one real second advances one game minute: a full day lasts 24 minutes. Fast and Very Fast shorten that to eight and three minutes. Pause whenever you want time to plan; walking keeps its physical pace while the daily clock leaves more room for household routines.
The live HUD keeps mood legible: the selected Lifelet wears a floating gem tinted by their current emotion, the portrait carries a matching mood ring, an emotion pill shows the feeling and its intensity, and moodlet tiles beside the portrait explain what is driving it; each need row explains itself on hover, and clicking it queues the fix through the same chooser autonomy uses.

Start with **New game**, create up to 8 Lifelets, choose their age, gender (Female/Male), looks, faces, outfits, personality and aspirations, then find a home. The creator offers ten hairstyles (crop, bob, curls, ponytail, long, buzz, waves, bun, braids, topknot), five tops (casual shirt, jacket, cardigan, tee, hoodie), trousers or shorts, a **Build** slider from slender to fuller, a **Height** slider, and a shoes swatch row, plus colour swatches for skin, hair, eyes, tops and bottoms. The swatch rows show the full authored palettes — 12 skin tones, 10 hair colours (plus an elder's own 5-colour silvered set) and 8 eye colours — so every look the models and **Surprise me** can produce is also reachable by hand; every age stage has the whole set. Clicking a **wardrobe** (or a full-length mirror) opens a styling panel with Clothes, Hair, Makeup and Jewelry tabs: every top, hairstyle, hair and eye colour, lip and eye look, earring style and metal is shown on the Lifelet before it is kept, and one look price keeps the whole thing. Trying anything on is free. The creator's Style tab adds makeup and jewelry — a full lip and eye set for girls, a limited men's set for boys, and the same jewelry anyone may wear, previewed on the Lifelet as you choose — and the Wardrobe tab keeps five saved looks — Everyday, Formal, Athletic, Sleep and Party. Formal is a long coat and tie, Athletic a sleeveless tank, Sleep a wrapped robe and sash, and Party a festive wrap with a handkerchief hem — original Blender clothes, not the same five shirts recoloured. A wardrobe on every starter home lets a Lifelet change into any of those looks, or pick a specific everyday top, and the portrait follows the change. Going to bed, for a run, to work, or to dance also changes them into Sleep, Athletic, Formal or Party clothes; waking, finishing a run, and coming home from school or work put Everyday clothes back on. Child, teen, young adult, adult and elder stages have matching character models; children need a teen or adult housemate. Connections lets a household begin as housemates, siblings, partners, parents and children. Parents must be adults in an older age stage during creation. The creator's **Face** tab shapes eleven independent features — cheek fullness, jaw definition, eye spacing, nose width, and the signed nose length, lip fullness, brow arch, chin length, face length, mouth width and nose bridge controls — and all eleven are real geometry on every age and every body frame, from baby to elder. **My Lifelet → Family tree** shows the household’s generations; choose a portrait card’s name to read connections from that person’s perspective. Click furniture or a person to choose an activity. Each Lifelet walks to their destination before beginning. The household shares money and time, while each person has their own needs, skills, relationships and queue. Select a household chip to take control of another person.

Lifelets approaching activities can step aside to let a housemate pass while keeping their original instructions. When several safe sidesteps work, they compare the stepping distance and the passing route before choosing. This includes a housemate finishing a protected stair exit or following a same-floor walking instruction. A Lifelet heading toward the stairs can also yield to someone returning to a free furnishing, preserving the original upstairs journey and furnishing queue priority through saving and loading. Briefly observing a blocked approach gives housemates an opportunity to yield before choosing another detour. Occupied furnishings have a physical waiting queue, including nearby approaches around corners, preserved across saving and harmless Build edits. Automatic activity changes check the furnishing's availability. Some crowded routes still remain blocked; the [sidestep selection review](docs/REVIEWS/iteration_46_courtesy_ranking.md), [current-floor passage review](docs/REVIEWS/iteration_45_current_floor.md) and [activity-flow review](docs/REVIEWS/iteration_43_activity_flow.md) record the tested recoveries and remaining limits.

Furniture and pools are solid. A pool, hot tub or slide blocks everything its model covers at its chosen size and turning, and a medium or large piece blocks the size it really is. A Lifelet or pet that is refused a step by something it was not told about turns away and goes round it, and one that can find no way says which item to move - "Please move the Swimming pool blocking the path." - instead of "out of reach". A dog held up by furniture gives its errand up after twelve seconds, stands rather than trotting in place, and is lifted out of any piece set down on top of it. See [navigation and collision](docs/CONSTRUCTION_API.md) and [pets](docs/PETS.md); `tests/test_navigation_blockers.gd` and `tests/test_pet_stuck_recovery.gd` cover them.

**Phone → Adopt a child** lets an adult at home review three school-age Lifelets and choose one or two adult guardians. Confirmation costs ℒ1,000 and requires a free household place; canceling keeps the family and funds unchanged. The child walks home from the street, joins the family tree and becomes controllable immediately. Their first school day is due after the settling-in day. You can pause and save during arrival while other household members keep their existing activities.

**Growing a family.** Click a double bed and choose **Assign bed sides…** to assign one adult to **Left Side** and another to **Right Side**. Then select each Lifelet, click that bed and choose **Go to Bed**. Each walks to their own bedside and sleeps on the assigned half, regardless of who arrives first. Assignments survive saving; keep both bedside paths clear. Partners can also share an unassigned bed automatically. With both partners asleep in the same bed, the bed offers **Try for Baby** to an opposite-gender pair of grown-ups: the woman must be a Young Adult or Adult, and her partner may be a Young Adult, Adult or Elder man. Either partner can start it from the bed. An Elder woman cannot carry a baby, and the bed says so in plain words; a partner who has passed on is refused too. An Elder father never hands his grey hair to the baby. The covers ruffle over them for a few minutes and either partner can stop it by clicking the bed again. If it finishes, the pair is expecting: a chime plays, a notice names the mother, and a **Pregnancy** moodlet with the countdown rides her moodlets while a pregnancy meter under her needs fills toward the birth. A growing baby bump shows on her model as the term advances. A second Try for Baby is refused, and the baby arrives about fourteen game days later. On the due day the household hears **Congratulations, mom is having a baby!**, then the **baby creator** opens — first name, the mother's surname by default, traits and appearance — before mom goes to the hospital. The partner chooses **Send Dad to Hospital** or **Notify Dad**, and **Welcome Baby Home** drives the household car onto the lot so mom steps out holding the swaddled newborn. The baby is already serialized into the household when that arrival starts, and stays in the save after welcome-home. A newborn is swaddled; after fourteen days they sit up with mat and rattle play; twelve days later they are a toddler who crawls, uses the potty, dollhouse and child desk. Needs still decay and they cry when low. Caregivers can **Pick Up for Cuddle**, **Talk To**, **Change Nappy**, spin a baby mobile, and feed from fridge bottles or jars (**Prepare Bottle** / **Get Baby Food**). The roll happens at conception, so two pregnancies can go differently; saving during the wait is safe. A born baby is named after the family: the child takes their mother's own surname when she has one. The baby appears in the life box beside everyone else, as a chip you click to select and control, and they are still there — chipped and controllable — after a save and a reload.

Children and teens have a **School** panel. **Go to school** sends them to class on weekdays, 08:00–15:00; arrival after 09:00 counts as late, with departures allowed until noon. Homework prepares the next attended class; lateness and absences affect grades. Adults can **Go to work** on weekdays, 09:00–17:00, with lateness after 10:00 and pay proportional to time at work. With a household car, **Go to work** walks the adult to the car, opens the door, seats them, closes the door, and drives away. In the evening the car returns and parks before the adult gets out, closes the door, walks around the house, and enters through the back doorway. The starter house includes that rear entrance. A house without a clear rear doorway is entered by the front door instead, a spot beside the car that is blocked by furniture or a neighbouring car is stepped around, and a walk that cannot finish is given up after a minute and a half rather than leaving anyone standing by the car. Without a car, the street walking commute remains available. Work and school include rest breaks that keep Energy at 80 or above through the return journey, leaving room for evening activities. You can control other household members while they are away, save and resume an absence, or bring someone home early without attendance/pay credit. School and career records offer online classes and home shifts as optional alternatives that share the same daily credit. A home shift is a real six-hour desk activity, and if a pressing need pulls the Lifelet away mid-shift the unfinished shift waits on the queue and resumes from where it stopped — the progress bar continues rather than restarting the shift.

**Cars drive like cars.** A car leaving for work or on a trip drives forward off its spot, turns smoothly onto the lane that matches its heading (the street keeps left: eastbound by the kerb, westbound across the road) and leaves along it, its front wheels steering and every wheel rolling. A car facing away from the street backs out in one curve instead. Coming home it drives along the road, turns off and pulls onto its own spot exactly where and how it was parked, forward when that is possible and otherwise by driving past and backing in. Cars never clip the house, furniture, fences or hedges; a fence is crossed only through a gate. Two cars that would meet take turns, the second waiting parked with a caption. The street is now two proper lanes wide. See [vehicle routing](docs/VEHICLE_ROUTING.md); `tests/test_vehicle_planner.gd`, `tests/test_commute_drive.gd`, `tests/test_trip_drive.gd` and `tests/test_vehicle_convoy.gd` cover it.

**Phone → Juniper Pet Shop** lets a household adopt a cat (ℒ320) or a dog (ℒ480) without leaving home. A shop visit reviews six animals that always include both species and both sexes; opening a review shows the pet picker, where you choose the species, the sex, the name, a coat built from two of 18 natural colours with a **mixed gradient** slider between them, the coat length, and markings (plain, bicolour, tuxedo, tabby, points or mask), with a live preview and a **Surprise me** reroll that keeps the species and sex you chose. The chosen price is charged once at confirmation and canceled visits change nothing; the pet then walks in from the street like an adopted child. The picker also chooses the **collar** and **leash** colours from their own swatch rows, and both are carried in the save, so two animals with the same coat are still told apart. Clicking a pet in the home opens its card. Accessories — bowls, cat trees, kennels, an indoor cat bed and dog bed, the feather mouse and knotted rope bone, and the ℒ50 **Cat Toy Box** and **Dog Toy Box**, each painted with its own paw print and holding six toys — are ordinary furnishings under the **Pets** catalogue category — a **Food & water bowl** (ℒ60) for any pet, a **Climbing cat tree** (ℒ240) for a cat and a **Garden dog kennel** (ℒ320) for a dog — and the shop only offers what the household can use. See [the pets guide](docs/PETS.md).

The school bus wears clean white paint, a green stripe and a three-leaf shamrock on each side. Tinted passenger windows and a wide split windshield reveal the seats inside; the front has a slatted grille and dark bumper, and four rear brake lamps brighten during pickup and drop-off. Children use the curb-side door and exit markers.

**Phone → Household calendar** brings school, work and expected birthdays together. Choose a day in the next two weeks and filter by category. Today's plans show queued, active, away and completed attendance. Birthdays follow the current aging settings; school and work forecasts stop at each Lifelet's next age change and refresh afterward. The calendar pauses play while open and restores your previous speed when closed.

Autonomy plans school, work and homework around urgent needs while preserving queued player instructions. During available school/work preparation, Lifelets prefer a brief fun break before starting a long canvas, and once the day is due they still take a break that ends in time to reach the lot exit by 10:00 for work or 09:00 for school; only pastimes that fit that deadline are offered, so the pastime rotation cannot make anyone late (a critically bored Lifelet, Fun under 12, still takes the shortest pastime and accepts a few late minutes), and the last three pastimes are saved with the household. Bookworms keep their reading preference. Pastimes spread across the whole home: a furnishing nobody in the household has used for half a day draws the next bored Lifelet, one everybody used in the last two hours is a little less tempting, the last five pastimes are remembered, and a soak in the tub counts as a pastime when hygiene is under 70. You can still direct any available activity. School records follow a Lifelet into later age stages. **My Lifelet → Celebrate a birthday** advances one stage after a confirmed ℒ30 activity. **Esc → Life settings** changes lifespan or pauses automatic birthdays for the household. When an elder finishes their last stage they pass on at home — even if they try to stay busy or away forever. Going too long without food, or collapsing on a run, can also end a life at any age. They remain as a gentle spirit who no longer works, cooks, flirts or starts a family. The household receives an inspectable keepsake and a small estate gift, and a garden remembrance stone is placed so the family can stand with it and remember. Coming home from sleep, school or work puts Everyday clothes back on.

**Retiring.** An elder who has been an elder for 14 game days (7 on a short lifespan, 56 on a long one; calendar days, counted even while automatic birthdays are paused) can retire. One who holds a job (has been paid for at least one shift) gets a corner notice and a letter, the Career panel's **Career details** button becomes **Retire…**, and the Career record has a **Retire…** button that asks first; until they choose, they keep working as before. An elder who has never been paid for a shift has no job to give up and retires on their own on that day. Retiring plays the big celebration, a loud fanfare with large streamers and **<Name> is now retired** in the middle of the screen with a "no longer required to work" line beneath it, and it never pauses the game. A retired Lifelet is no longer asked to work: **Go to work**, **Drive to work** and home shifts are refused with a reason, the way to work leaves the neighbourhood exit menu, no shift is counted as missed, the household calendar lists no work for them, and **Find a job** is closed for good; freelance work at a computer stays open as a hobby. A pension of ℒ1,000 arrives every 7 days, the first one 7 days after retiring, with a corner notice, and it is never paid twice across a save and a load or after the Lifelet passes on. Retiring is one-way: only **Change Age** to a younger stage on the farewell dialog clears it. The Career panel then reads **Retired** with the pension and its next day, and the age tooltip says how long is left before an elder can retire. `tests/test_retirement.gd` (rules, pension timing, saves, older-save migration) and `tests/test_retirement_celebration.gd` (the rendered banner, the Retire button and a reload) cover it.

At a desk, **Do homework together…** lets a child or teen learn with a trusted household adult. Both walk into position, share progress, and gain friendship and learning when they finish. The helper builds Parenting skill. Canceling from either participant ends the shared activity; saving preserves unfinished work. The HUD identifies the partner, and skill bars show progress toward the next level. Short completion messages appear in readable cards beside their speakers; the selected Lifelet has priority when a scene is crowded.

**Cook a fresh meal** opens a cookbook: garden skillet makes four servings for ℒ12, herb garden pasta unlocks at Cooking 2 and makes four for ℒ16, mushroom soup unlocks at Cooking 3 and makes four for ℒ20, harvest vegetable bake unlocks at Cooking 4 and makes eight for ℒ24, and berry crumble unlocks at Cooking 5 and makes six for ℒ28. Each recipe lists its preparation time. Ingredients are charged once when cooking begins; the cook carries the finished dish to a table, and eating restores hunger. Click the served dish to **Call everyone to eat**, take a serving, **Ask who wants food…** and offer each Lifelet a serving by name (someone full, tired or busy says no), put the uneaten servings back in the fridge, or **Throw it in the bin**. Food resting on a table is clicked directly — the plate sits inside the table's own pick volume, so it is picked on its own before the furniture — and **Clear the table** gathers and washes every plate left on it. Each diner uses a separate plate and available chair. When seats are unavailable, Lifelets walk to reserved standing places that remain clear of other settled activities; building over a dining place reroutes the same partial serving. Fridge leftovers remain selectable until they spoil. Finished meals leave dirty plates that can be carried to a sink and washed. Idle Lifelets may wash dishes after tending their needs and responsibilities. Dishes retain clear support when meals end or furniture is sold; idle Lifelets step aside when crowding another person. Partial meals and their ownership survive saving, and furniture moves preserve the same food. Pasta and bake use original dishes, plates and preparation gestures. The bake now uses an opening oven door and sliding rack: the Lifelet seasons, loads, bakes and retrieves the dish before serving. Paused saves restore the dish and pose during transfer or inside the oven, preserving the one ingredient charge and later instructions.

Priya and Tom join Maya and Leo on the lane: four neighbors with their own homes, walk rhythms, traits and weekday routines. Click a passing neighbor to talk; reaching 20 friendship opens **Visit home** in People and their address in **Explore**. Conversations need a clear line: two Lifelets on opposite sides of a wall do not chat through it, and a passing neighbor only stops for a member they can see. When the household comes home from a trip, each Lifelet steps out at a curb spot that is clear of furniture and the others, so nobody returns standing inside a chair or behind a body. Priya lives in a lamplit flat with a window-seat study nook; Tom's fence-line house leans into its garden. Leo has a wider bungalow with a reading corner and terrace, and Maya a garden cottage with a planted porch. Their floor plans and furnishings differ. The whole household walks to a shared car, drives across town and arrives outside. Travel advances 15 game minutes; saving becomes available on arrival. Wait for anyone at school or work to return before travelling together. Visits preserve your own home, friendships and resident positions.

Neighbors keep weekday routines: Priya reads at the library and Tom tends his garden plot on weekday days, so their homes stand empty and their usual haunts stay occupied until the window closes. Reading at the library or relaxing at the garden while a routine resident is there builds that friendship a little on its own.

**The street has people of every age.** Children, a teenager, an adult walking a dog on a lead, an elder and a dog trotting by pass the front sidewalk at the hours their kind of person would be out, each at a natural pace (an elder about 0.75 m/s, a child 0.95, an adult 1.1, a teen 1.2, a dog 1.25) with feet that keep their footing at every game speed. A Lifelet who can see the sidewalk (out on the lot, or at a door or window with a clear line) can click any passer for a short menu: **Wave hello**, **Say hello**, a small-talk question that suits them, **Compliment their dog** for the dog walker, and **Say hello to the dog** or **Pet the dog** for a dog. The Lifelet walks to a clear spot beside them, the passer stops, turns to face them and answers, and Social and Fun rise a little more each time the same face is met. The same passer will not chat again for a while, and the passer walks on the moment the chat ends, however it ends. An idle Lifelet outside who is short of company sometimes starts one on their own.

**A hug is a real embrace.** The hugger walks up, both Lifelets turn to face each other and step in until their chests meet, wrap their arms round each other's backs, hold with heads together, and step apart. A housemate who is walking stops for it; a neighbour returns it; an adult bends to hug a child. Cancelling or saving mid-hug lets both go at once.

Nobody wanders in. A neighbor reaching your front path rings the **doorbell** and waits on the doorstep, outside the house; a card offers **Let them in** or **Ask them to leave**, and a caller turned away stays away for the day. Letting them in walks them through the ordinary welcome path, and **Say goodbye** ends the visit whenever you like. From **People**, choose **Invite over** to bring a known neighbor to your home. When they arrive, choose **Welcome in**; your selected Lifelet finishes earlier activities, greets the guest, and lets them inside. The guest card shows how long they will wait or stay. **Say goodbye** sends them home after any conversation already underway. One guest can visit at a time, and building or travelling waits until they leave. Visit progress survives a named save, including an unfinished welcome.

**School's out, homework's on.** A child or teen who boards the school bus has a real school day. The bus comes once on a school morning, waits at the curb until the pupils have boarded (no later than 09:00, or 09:20 for a pupil already walking out) and drives away, then stays away all school day. It comes back once at the end of school and sets the pupils down one at a time, each stepping off beside it and walking straight in through the front door to a different spot just inside; then it leaves until the next school morning. There is no bus at weekends or for a home with no pupils, and a pupil turned back at the bus door by an urgent need stays home without sending it off early. Coming home from school early ends indoors too. The day ends at 15:00 with attendance, and once indoors they go straight to somewhere to sit and do their homework — they look first for their own desk and chair, then a table with a chair, then a desk; the bookshelf is only the fallback, used when no child desk, table or chair stands in the home, and a seat that is busy is waited for rather than swapped for the shelf; with nowhere to sit at all they play rather than stand idle (a second pupil who finds every chair at the table taken stands at it). Saves from the earlier build, in which boarding the bus left a child away with nothing queued, are mended on load. **Toys have somewhere to live.** A toy is in its box, in a hand or on the floor, and the saved layout, the node and the box always agree: nothing pops out of a box on a load, an undo or a move, a sold or stored box takes its toys with it, and a toy is never left floating in the air or hidden outside a box. - **Your life saves itself while you play** ([test](tests/test_autosave.gd)) — every five minutes by default, and on any interval you choose in **Life settings** (off, or 1 to 60 minutes). The chosen interval rides the save and comes back on load. 16/16 checks, including a real write and a fresh-process reload.
