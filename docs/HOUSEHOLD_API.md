# Household coordination

`LifeHousehold` owns up to eight independent `LifeSim` nodes. `new_household(profiles)` creates them, with IDs `player`, `housemate_1`, and onward. Public `members` entries contain `{id,sim}`. `selected()`, `selected_id()`, `select(index)`, and `member_sim(id)` expose selection.

Call `tick(delta)` once for the household. Do not separately tick its members. Time and speed are shared; needs, skills, careers, moods, memories and action queues remain individual. Money is synchronized before and after each member step. Only the first Lifelet charges the household's daily bill. UI edits to the selected member's funds/speed are adopted on the next tick or `adopt_selected_changes()`; `set_funds()` and `set_speed()` apply immediately to all members.

`member_action_started(id,action)` and `member_action_finished(id,action)` identify whose movement/animation to update. World integration must keep a path per member and call `begin_action(id)` on arrival so ingredient charges update the shared wallet. `register_targets(array)` supplies autonomy targets to everyone. `notice(message)` forwards household events.

`save_game(world_data)` stores every member, selection, shared resources, and the world snapshot in the existing user save path. `load_game()` returns `{ok,world}` or `{ok:false,error}` and accepts the original single-Lifelet save format. `restore_state()` validates every member before replacing any live state. Positions remain world-owned and should be stored alongside the world data or in each character's `world_state`.

`tests/test_household.gd`: 20 stateful assertions covering concurrent activity, independent skills/needs, one wallet, one daily bill, exact pause, selection, migration, and atomic restore rejection.

## Shared resources and waiting movement

The world controller in `scripts/main.gd` owns resource admission. `_activity_resources(action)` includes the target object, linked resources such as a desk's supporting chair, and the activity's standing cells. `_activity_available(action)` gives an active occupant priority, then the oldest arrived waiter. Arrival uses absolute game minutes, `(day - 1) * 1440 + minutes`; equal timestamps break ties by member ID. An actor finishing an activity cannot repeatedly overtake an older waiter. Participants with the same cooperative session ID share their existing reservation.

Per-member `motion_states` hold `waiting`, `wait_started`, `wait_review`, `wait_destination` and `resume_active` alongside the normal path. Contended actors walk to separate nearby positions with navigation and body-clearance checks. The oldest waiter keeps their reservation while walking back to the object, and begins only on arrival. Cancellation and target removal clear the position and reservation. This separates settled waiting bodies; it does not introduce general collision avoidance for every pair of moving actors.

`save_game` records `resource_wait_started`, `resource_action_active`, `waiting_action_id` and `waiting_target_id` inside each character's `world_state`. `_restore_resource_waits()` reinstates valid arrived reservations before movement. An existing active owner keeps the object through their restored approach; otherwise the required `approach` phase on load would let waiting newcomers take their paid activity. Old saves without the active flag infer an unfinished owner from the existing paid flag when no arrived wait was saved. Active-owner priority applies only to the restored current action, not unrelated availability queries. Normal queue payment and elapsed progress remain owned by LifeSim.

`tests/test_resource_fairness.gd` covers arrival order, repeat requests, ties, cancellation, seven distinct waiting positions, physical return, target removal, restored/legacy occupancy and cooperative resource sharing (71 checks). `python tests/run_playthrough.py --suite wait_priority` performs public eight-member creation, seven real same-bed waits, a named save, process restart, occupancy restoration and public cancellation admitting the oldest waiter (154 + 25 checks). `--suite autonomy_week --timeout 600` keeps the unchanged one-bed starter home and observes a real seven-day clock with a fresh-process midpoint load. Its gates inspect every member's completed work, all intercompletion gaps and stationary approaches. The trace also records wait timestamps and positions; `tests/summarize_autonomy.py` reports settled waiting overlaps. # Creator family connections and genealogy

`configure_family(links: Array) -> Dictionary` accepts the complete creator draft and returns `{ok:true}` or `{ok:false,error:String}`. Roles are `housemates`, `siblings`, `partners`, and `parent`. A parent declaration is directed: `{"a":"player","b":"housemate_1","role":"parent"}` means `player` is a parent of `housemate_1`. It validates detached snapshots before changing any live graph, relationship, currency or Node. Existing `LifeSim` references remain stable. Setup is restricted to an idle, fresh day-one 08:00 household with no social history.

A declared parent must be an adult in a strictly later age stage than the child. A young adult can parent a child or teen; an elder can parent an adult. Same-stage parent/child creation is unavailable because the game does not yet record chronological ages. This rule is checked only when the relationship is created. Existing parent/child ties remain valid when independent birthdays bring both Lifelets into the same stage or the child reaches a later stage. A Lifelet has at most two declared parents, and ancestry cannot contain cycles or contradictory ancestor/sibling roles.

Names never infer relationships. Parent links do not imply a partnership, and a parent's partner does not automatically become the child's other parent. These declarations can represent biological or adoptive family without guessing either history. A starting partnership still requires two unrelated adults and permits only one partner per Lifelet. Setup grants no money or earned social memories.

Explicit sibling links form a connected group, preserving the previous A–B/B–C sibling behavior. A shared parent also establishes siblings, including half-siblings. A chain of half-siblings with different parents does not make its otherwise unrelated endpoints siblings. Contradictory explicit housemate or partner choices between derived relatives are rejected with a reason to revise the other connections first.

## Reading the family

- `family_relationship(a:String,b:String) -> String` describes **b relative to a**. For a parent edge a→b it returns `child`; the reverse call returns `parent`.
- Roles are `none`, `siblings`, `parent`/`child`, `grandparent`/`grandchild`, `ancestor`/`descendant`, `parent_sibling`/`sibling_child`, `cousin`, and `relative`. The labels for the nongendered extended roles are “Parent’s sibling” and “Sibling’s child”. `LifeFamilyGraph.label(role)` supplies display text, and `inverse(role)` supplies the reciprocal role.
- `get_family_links() -> Array` returns minimal explicit parent/sibling declarations plus current household partner pairs. It omits housemate pairs and all derived grandparents, cousins, and other relatives. Partner links follow the current `bond`, so breakups disappear from this editable edge list.
- `family_parent_links() -> Array` returns only `{a:parent_id,b:child_id,role:"parent"}` edges for drawing the directed family tree.
- `LifeFamilyGraph.create(profiles_by_id:Dictionary,links:Array) -> Dictionary` provides a detached graph builder: `{ok,graph,partners}` or `{ok:false,error}`. `relationship(graph,a,b)`, `validate(graph,member_ids)`, `canonical(graph,member_ids)`, and `links(graph)` are pure graph helpers.

Derived kinship roles are cached until membership or validated family declarations change. Use the household APIs to replace these owned values; do not mutate `family_graph` directly. Scores, partners and age eligibility still synchronize each tick. The cache is rebuilt from the graph after restoration and is never serialized. See `HOUSEHOLD_PERFORMANCE.md` for measured timings and the 46 behavioral invalidation checks.

## Persistence and social safeguards

Household state includes `family_graph: {version:1,parents:[{a,b}],siblings:[{a,b}]}`. The graph contains only explicit kinship edges; live partnerships stay in the existing reciprocal `romantic_partner` and relationship `bond` fields. Derived `relationships[target_id].family_role` fields are persisted too and must agree with the graph. Parent/child and other directional roles must have correct inverses, rather than identical strings.

Loading validates the graph, canonical member identities, complete reciprocal relationship roles, social history, and queued actions before replacing live Nodes. The input dictionary is deep-copied before migration, so failed or successful loads do not mutate caller data. Legacy saves without a graph may contain the earlier symmetric sibling roles; they migrate to minimal sibling declarations. Implied legacy siblings receive derived roles while retaining numeric/social history, allowing validation to reject contradictory romance rather than silently erasing it. Saves containing new directed roles without their graph are rejected. Familial roles assigned to neighbors outside the household are also rejected because this graph cannot represent outside relatives yet.

All known kin roles block flirting, partnership, commitment and breakup actions, including after both relatives are adults. Validation rejects familial romance, partnership bonds, romantic milestones/history, and romantic queued actions. Friendly interactions remain available; mirrored social results preserve target-specific parent/child roles and labels. Runtime graph context prevents a stale copied relationship role from opening a romantic action.
