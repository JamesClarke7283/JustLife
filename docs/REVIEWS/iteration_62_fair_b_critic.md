## What improved

The repeated diagonal lower-cheek dents are substantially quieter. Marin
(00), Rowan (01), Wren (04) and Avery (05) make the change especially clear
against their older views. Cheek-to-jaw shading is more continuous, and the
faces look less damaged. The broad/short and narrow/long face distinctions
remain visible rather than being smoothed into one outline.

This justifies a modest art-score increase from the prior review's 6.5
band. It does not justify an individuality increase: the pass removes
artifacts, but the shared central facial design remains. Presentation also
stays at 8.0: the calm palette, readable grouping and large portrait work
well, but they are substantially unchanged.

## Ranked remaining issues

1. **Finish the remaining chin and feature transitions.** Small dark,
   pinched wedges remain under the chin in the hero and in Rowan/Avery's
   three-quarter views. Some residual lower-cheek unevenness is still
   visible in three-quarter. The nose's bridge/sidewall and nostril base
   read more abruptly than the improved cheeks; the lips retain a thin,
   sharply outlined, applied-on appearance. Eye openings are better
   enclosed than the earlier baseline but still lack a convincing upper
   lid edge and corner design. Use targeted form/transition correction,
   not indiscriminate whole-face smoothing. Acceptance: no repeated
   chin pinch, with deliberate nose, mouth and lid transitions across
   the same six faces in both angles.

2. **Make the central features distinguish people, not just their face
   lengths.** Marin's broader lower face and mouth, Devon's longer face,
   and Finley's shorter nose are readable. Devon/Wren remain close, and
   the rounder faces converge in three-quarter. The same eye opening,
   nose-tip family and lip contour dominate recognition. Coherent eye
   aperture/tilt, jaw-angle, bridge-to-tip and lip-contour variants would
   matter more than stronger slider numbers. Acceptance should be an
   uninvolved review of shuffled, same-style portraits at normal zoom;
   that recognition test has not been performed here.

3. **Raise the weaker surrounding assets to the face's new baseline.**
   Buzz in these images still reads as a thick smooth cap with a raised
   front rim. The shirt has inflated shoulders and deep sleeve/body
   joins, with little visible garment construction. These occupy enough
   of the portrait to hold back whole-character quality even when the
   cheeks improve. Judge the pending hair and shirt work from its own
   matched evidence; do not award it points in advance. The hero Bob is
   stronger, though its heavy lower edge remains conspicuous.

4. **Give the skin restrained, intentional material variation.** Broad
   gradients are cleaner, but the face still reads fairly uniform and
   clay-like at normal portrait size. More purposeful, subtle complexion
   and roughness variation around cheeks, nose and lips would help
   distinguish skin from the hair, eyes and clothing. Extra pores or
   surface noise alone would not solve this. Check the result on several
   skin tones and lighting angles before calling the material finished.

**Decision:** useful facial-surface cleanup; retain it subject to runtime
checks. Individuality and the remaining visible finish gaps are not solved,
and no score is raised merely to approach the requested goal.
