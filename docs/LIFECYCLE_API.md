# Lifecycle implementation checkpoint

`LifeLifecycle` owns age labels, original stage durations, legacy migration and save validation. `LifeSim.lifecycle` stores fractional stage progress, short/normal/long pace, automatic birthdays and chronological transition history. Normal stage lengths are 20/21/28/42/28 days for child/teen/young adult/adult/elder; short is half and long four times those durations. These are JustLife balance choices, not a claim about current Sims 4 tuning.

The simulation advances age by elapsed game minutes. Pause and disabled automatic birthdays stop that progress. Changing pace preserves the fraction of the current stage, and sequential birthdays preserve traits, skills and relationships. An explicit birthday is a queued 45-minute fridge activity costing ℒ30. Its confirmation states the next stage before queuing; cancellation of the confirmation preserves age and money. The actor holds an original Blender cake, blows out its candles and applauds. Independent normal-game evidence verifies the held cake, three lit candles, extinguished flames, applause and eventual age change; see `REVIEWS/iteration_07_presentation.md`.

`age_stage` describes chronological appearance. `life_stage` continues to express existing adult/minor/unknown interaction eligibility. Child and teen map to minor; young adult, adult and elder map to adult. Household birthdays update reciprocal eligibility, emit `member_age_changed`, and refresh the affected actor and portrait. Old adult saves migrate to young adult; old minor saves migrate to teen without granting adult eligibility. Invalid or inconsistent age/history data is rejected before the live household is changed.

Public controls: **Creator → Age**, **Esc → Life settings**, and **My Lifelet → Celebrate a birthday**. Creator age options become available only when the actor reports support for their authored models. UI cameras can use actor-local portrait center and display height. Children need a teen or adult in their household to move in.

This is an incomplete lifecycle checkpoint: full generational play and inheritance remain required work. An elder whose stage has run its course reaches the farewell dialog and passes on (`LifeLifecycle.due_to_pass_on`, `LifeSim.pass_on`), so elders do die; an elder can first retire (below). Schooling has a separate persistent module. This checkpoint does not establish full generational gameplay or a 10/10 rating.

Validation: `tests/test_lifecycle.gd` initially passed 27 assertions alongside all 259 pre-existing simulation/progression/household/story/save/relationship/family assertions. The independent school playthrough later passed 116 rendered/restart checks for public child/adult creation, real homework/classes, child-to-teen birthday/model replacement and school history persistence; see `REVIEWS/iteration_07.md`. The boundary review adds 62 adversarial checks for delayed actions, target mutation, birthday races and save observers. Late birthday enrollment defers the first possible class to the following day while permitting that evening's homework; 22 focused enrollment checks cover old-save migration and calendar validation.

## The day a stage began

Rules tied to how long someone has been a teenager or an elder count calendar days, so they keep counting when automatic birthdays are off. `LifeLifecycle.days_in_stage(state, stage, day)` answers in whole days. It reads the newest of two records: the `day` of the last birthday in `history` that reached the stage, and `lifecycle.stage_day`, an optional key the sim writes the first time it ticks or is saved (the household sets the shared clock only after a Lifelet is created), at every birthday and when Change Age picks a stage. A save from before `stage_day` takes it from its history when it loads; a Lifelet created partway through a stage with no history is dated from the stage progress. `LifeLifecycle.scaled_days(normal_days, lifespan)` scales an age-tied number of days with the lifespan setting (short is half, long is four times); fixed spans such as a seven-day course do not use it. `LifeLifecycle.milestone_text(first_name, stage)` is the centre-banner wording: "Kit is now a teenager!".

Change Age on the farewell dialog (`LifeSim.cancel_pending_passing`) rewrites the birthday history (`history_after_change`) and starts a fresh school record that keeps earlier terms, so the save always loads. It runs the same `_on_stage_entered(previous, next, source)` hook as a birthday, with source `"change_age"`, and sends no milestone.

## Milestones and the centre banner

`LifeSim.celebrate_birthday(start_next_action, source)` takes a source word (`"auto"` when the lifespan clock runs out, `"cake"` for the paid fridge celebration) and keeps it in `last_birthday_source`. It leaves a hospital stay, a prison sentence and a driving lesson running instead of calling the Lifelet home, calls `_on_stage_entered` for features that begin with a stage, and sends `LifeSim.milestone(kind, data)`. A feature that has its own milestone sends it with `LifeSim._emit_milestone(kind, data)`. The household relays that as `LifeHousehold.member_milestone(member_id, kind, data)`, never while a save is being restored; `age_changed` and `member_age_changed` keep their two and three arguments. A birthday also posts the "Many happy returns" card to the post box.

`LifeAnnouncements` (`scripts/announcements.gd`, `main.announcements`) turns each milestone into what the player sees, one at a time, only in the house and with no pausing menu open, and drops everything waiting when a game is loaded:

| Kind | What the player sees |
| --- | --- |
| `birthday` | Grand banner and fanfare: "<First> is now <a child / a teenager / a young adult / an adult / an elder>!" for every stage change, automatic or paid. |
| `retired` | Grand banner: "<Full name> is now retired". |
| `retirement_eligible` | Corner notice and a letter. |
| `driving_introduced` | Card banner (no streamers, no fanfare) and a letter. |
| `driving_licensed` | Grand banner. |
| `pension` | Corner notice with the amount. |

`data.text` replaces the default notice or detail line. `LifeMilestoneCelebration` (`scripts/milestone_celebration.gd`) is the banner itself: a centred card, 240 streamers and confetti bursting from the bottom corners, and one fanfare player, all mouse-transparent and gone after seven seconds. `CelebrationAudio` makes the fanfare, the birthday melody (the public-domain 1893 tune, notes only) and an eight-second party loop from arithmetic, built once on a worker thread. `LifePartyMusic` plays the birthday tune and the party loop, turns the theme down by volume (never pauses it), follows the Sound and Music switches and holds still while the household is paused.

## Driving licence

`LifeDrivingSchool` (`scripts/driving_school.gd`) holds the rules and `LifeSim.driving` the record, saved under the optional sim key `driving`; see [DRIVING_SCHOOL.md](DRIVING_SCHOOL.md). Lessons open after `LifeLifecycle.scaled_days(20, lifespan)` calendar days as a teenager (`days_in_stage`) or on becoming a young adult. A Lifelet created as a young adult or older, or loaded from a save with no record, is licensed. A birthday keeps the record; Change Age (`_on_stage_entered` with source `change_age`) starts it afresh for the new stage. The `driving_introduced` and `driving_licensed` milestones are sent by `LifeSim._advance_driving` and `_tick_lesson_away`.

## Retirement and the pension

`LifeRetirement` (`scripts/retirement.gd`) holds the rules and `LifeSim.retirement` the record: `{version, eligible_noticed, retired, retired_day, next_pension_day, pension_paid}`. It saves under the optional sim key `retirement`; an older save has none and gets the fresh record. The record does not count elder days. That is `LifeLifecycle.days_in_stage(lifecycle, "elder", day)` from the section above, so a Lifelet created as an elder, one who grew into it and one whose age was set by Change Age are all dated the same way, in calendar days that keep counting with automatic birthdays off. An elder may retire after `LifeRetirement.needed_days(lifespan)` of them: 14 at the normal pace, 7 on a short life, 56 on a long one (`scaled_days(14, lifespan)`). The pension period (7 days) and amount (ℒ1,000) are fixed numbers.

`LifeSim._advance_retirement()` runs at every midnight, after the bill cycle. For a retired Lifelet it credits every pension that has fallen due and moves `next_pension_day` on in the same step, before any milestone goes out, so a save taken in a callback already holds the payment and a reload cannot pay a period twice. It pays nothing to a spirit. For an eligible elder it sends `retirement_eligible` once (`eligible_noticed`), or, when `has_job()` is false (the elder has never been paid for a shift), calls `retire()` itself. `retire()` is the one way in: it refuses while the Lifelet is away, imprisoned or in the middle of a work action, drops queued work, records `retired_day` and `next_pension_day = day + 7`, adds a "Happily retired" moodlet and a memory, and sends `retired` (`data.text` is the line under the banner). `LifeHousehold.retire_member(id)` is the call the Career record uses. The first pension is `retired_day + 7`.

A retired Lifelet is refused `career_day`, `drive_to_work` and `job` (`LifeRetirement.RETIRED_REASON`), `_autonomy_duty_id` and the lot-exit menu leave work out, `LifeCareerSchedule.advance` counts no missed shifts, the calendar lists no work, the criminal line of work is skipped, and `career_entry_error` closes Find a job. Freelance `work` stays open. Retirement is one way: `_on_stage_entered` resets the record whenever the stage changes, so only Change Age to another stage undoes it (extending the same stage keeps it).

`LifeRetirement.validate` runs when a save loads. It refuses a record that is not a dictionary with exactly the six keys, whole numbers that are not whole, version other than 1, a retired Lifelet who is not an elder, a retirement day outside `[1, day]` or before the elder stage began, a pension day off the 7-day cycle or beyond `day + 7`, `pension_paid` that does not equal ℒ1,000 times the periods already due, and a retirement that was never announced. It does not require `next_pension_day > day`, because a Lifelet who passed on stops being paid, and it does not tie the record to the current lifespan, so stretching the lifespan after retiring cannot break a save. `LifeSim._validate_state` also refuses a retired Lifelet with a queued `career_day`, `job` or `drive_to_work`, or one who is away at work.

## Gathering round the cake

`LifeBirthdayRitual` (`scripts/birthday_ritual.gd`) holds the rules and `LifeBirthdayFlow` (`scripts/birthday_flow.gd`, `main.birthday_flow`) shows them. Only a birthday whose `source` is `"auto"` owes a cake: `LifeHousehold._relay_milestone` notes it in `household.celebrations`, `{version, next_serial, pending: [{serial, member_id, from, to, day, minutes, party_serial, hold_until?}]}`. The paid `"cake"` birthday owes nothing. An entry is dropped after `PENDING_DAYS` (2) calendar days, or when its Lifelet has changed age or passed on, and it is saved under the optional household key `celebrations` (absent when nothing is owed, so older saves load and newer ones look the same when there is no birthday). `LifeBirthdayRitual.validate` refuses a record that is not a dictionary of the right shape, holds more than eight entries, repeats a Lifelet, skips a stage (`next_stage(from) == to`), names a Lifelet whose stage is no longer `to` or who has passed on, numbers an entry past `next_serial`, or dates it outside the last two days.

The gathering is a cooperative session of kind `"birthday"`, built like the group dance: one clock owned by the birthday person's `blow_candles` action, a `sing_birthday` action for each singer, each with its own standing spot, and a token that starts `birthday_`. Neither action can be queued by hand (`queue_action` refuses them), and both are in the saved-action checks (`cooperation_role` `celebrant` or `singer`, `cooperation_primary` true only for the celebrant, `target_kind` `birthday_cake`). The session is `{id, kind, celebrant_id, serial, table_id, held, cake_position, day, created_minutes, phase, ready, waited, members (birthday person first), positions}`; `table_id` is `""` and `held` true when the cake is in two hands. `LifeHousehold.birthday_plan(id)` says who would gather now and who is busy; `queue_birthday_gathering(...)` starts it (autonomous fronts are cancelled, what the player had lined up is put behind the song and begins again afterwards); `birthday_sessions()`, `birthday_session_for(id)` and `cooperative_presentation(member)` read it. A singer who cancels leaves the others singing. The birthday person cancelling, a new day, or nobody arriving within `WAIT_LIMIT` (30 game minutes) calls it off, and the owed entry gets a `hold_until` ninety minutes on. Singers who are still on their way when the wait runs out are dropped and the song starts without them. Finishing pays each person (`LifeSim._apply_birthday_result`: "Best birthday ever" when at least two sang, otherwise "Make a wish"; "Birthday wishes" for singers; a memory), adds four friendship each way between the birthday person and every singer, clears the owed entry and sends `birthday_ritual_finished`, which the flow answers by setting a `layer_cake` batch of eight servings on the table.

The timeline in game minutes (a minute is a second at Normal speed): sing 0 to 17 (as long as the tune), hush to 18.5, the candles go out and the birthday person blows to 19.5, then everyone cheers until 26. `LifeBirthdayRitual.member_error` is the one test of who is free: living, not a baby, at home, not already in a session, not asleep, napping or heading out for school or work, not within `DUTY_MARGIN` (45) minutes of having to leave (`LifeSim.minutes_until_departure`), and not in the middle of something the player chose. It never looks at homework. The window is 07:00 to 21:30. The order when things want the same Lifelet is leaving for school or work, a booked driving lesson, the birthday gathering, a teen's homework, the player's own plans, then the Lifelet's own choices.

`LifeBirthdayFlow` starts a waiting gathering when the house is calm (live, at home, clock running, no menu open), picks the table (a dining table with a festive cloth, then any dining table, coffee table or worktop, nearest the birthday person; a stove top never) and the standing spots round it (`ring_slots`, each snapped to clear, reachable floor within 2.2 m and at least 0.55 m from the next), or a held cake with a ring round the birthday person (`held_offsets`) when the table cannot take everyone. While the session runs it keeps the cake on the table (the table's own bowl of fruit makes way for it, through the `BirthdayCake` node `LifeWorld.sync_surface_decorations` looks for), sets `LifeActor.celebration_presentation` on everyone (role, ritual phase, elapsed game minutes, held cake, cake point), plays the tune through `LifePartyMusic` from the session's elapsed seconds (so a loaded game resumes it where it was), slows the game to Normal speed and puts the player's speed back afterwards, and gives the singers ♪ bubbles, the birthday person a wish and everyone cheers. After a load it simply finds the session again and rebuilds all of it.
